# Market generator failure modes — WGAN-GP on 17-symbol × ~1250-bar log returns

**Research decision for: OpenTrader market generator (DoppelGANger-style WGAN-GP).**
**Date: 2026-08-05.**
**Status: literature review against primary sources (arXiv abs pages / full text / official code repos); every claim below was verified against the listed source this session. A second training run is in flight with the already-applied fixes (per-window standardization, S=5 batch generation, stride-16 windows, WGAN-GP λ=10, Adam 5e-4 (0.5, 0.999), GRU critic, metadata critic α=0.5) — assume it may still fail, and treat §0 as the diagnosis order for that run.**

## 0. Ranked fix list (most impactful first)

1. **DoppelGANger auto-normalization: learn per-window (min,max) as generated metadata and rescale after generation — don't just zero-center.** Mode collapse driven by range variability is called out as *the* reason naive time-series GANs drift to a wrong level/mean ([1909.13403] §3.3.2, §4.2); a fixed zero-mean/unit-std transform does not remove it (the same transform is applied to every sample, so the collapse problem is unchanged — DG §4.2). This is the highest-probability root cause of the 12× mean drift.
2. **Add TimeGAN's supervised one-step-ahead loss (embedding + supervisor) on top of the adversarial loss.** The only mechanism in the literature that *directly* targets short-lag temporal dynamics is a supervised next-step prediction in a learned embedding space (TimeGAN, NeurIPS 2019); adversarial-only training leaves sequence structure largely unenforced (Sig-WGAN: GANs "struggle to capture the temporal dependence of joint probability distributions induced by time-series data" [2111.01207]).
3. **Switch the critic away from per-sample WGAN-GP-only training: TTUR (separate, D>G learning rates) + R1 zero-centered gradient penalty (or SN), and check for critic saturation.** WGAN-GP with a finite number of critic updates per generator step is *proven* not to always converge ([1801.04406]); a saturated critic gives the generator an uninformative gradient, which is exactly when a GRU generator falls back to its prior: drift + near-i.i.d. rows + zero cross-asset correlation.
4. **Generate the 17 symbols as one joint vector with a cross-sectional structure (factor-style), and evaluate/criticize the correlation matrix explicitly.** Independent-per-symbol or jointly-but-unstructured generation collapses cross-asset correlation; the mechanism is documented in [2410.09850] (bivariate GBM: real corr 0.899 → generated 0.825, bivariate distribution "circular") and the fix in [2601.17773] (single joint return vector + factor inductive bias; model selection on correlation-matrix Frobenius error).
5. **Move the tail problem out of the network: Lambert W × F_X pre/post-processing on log returns (QuantGAN §5.3).** A Gaussian-driven neural process has all moments finite, so it *cannot* learn heavy tails; the verified fix is to whiten tails before training and invert after generation ([1907.06673] §5.3). If tails are still under-sampled after that, use a tail-risk objective (Tail-GAN's VaR/ES elicitability [2203.01664]) instead of tuning the GAN harder.

Also do this **before** ranking any further tweaks: split the autocorrelation error into **ACF of returns, ACF of squared returns, ACF of absolute returns** (the QuantGAN protocol, §7.4 of [1907.06673]). An ACF error of 0.69 on returns-only data is not what any of the referenced papers report for a working generator; the 0.69-vs-0.02 gap is plausibly measuring the wrong object (e.g. mixing squared-return clustering into the returns ACF, or measuring the ACF of level paths where the drift term dominates).

## 1. Why GANs trained on financial/economic time series fail

### 1.1 (a) Amplified drift / wrong marginal mean

- **Mode collapse on range variability.** DoppelGANger's central diagnosis: in time series, "mode collapse is particularly exacerbated... because of the high variability in the range of measurement values", and crucially, "a standard normalization approach would be to normalize by the *global* min/max... this is just scaling and shifting by a constant; from the GAN's perspective, the learning problem is the same, so mode collapse still occurs" ([1909.13403] §3.3.2, §4.2). Their example: pages with >2000 views/day vs <10 views/day; a collapsed GAN reproduces only the high-drift/high-amplitude mode — the "12× mean" symptom. Their fix (auto-normalization, ranked #1 above) stores each series' range as *learned fake metadata* so every generated series is rescaled by a generated limit.
- **Compounding error along the path.** Even when the *marginal distribution of log returns* is learned well, "a small difference in return value between generated and original data results in a more significant difference as the error accumulates" — the JSD of *final values* grows sharply with sequence length while the return JSD stays flat ([2410.09850] §4.3.1). A tiny per-step mean bias is invisible to the critic but becomes a 12× cumulative drift in generated worlds.
- **RNN state accumulation.** RNN generators forget temporal correlation the more passes they take ("the more passes taken, the more temporal correlation RNNs tend to forget", [1909.13403] §4.1); conversely the TCN generator used by QuantGAN is constructed so that the induced process is stationary by design — "it is particularly suited for modeling long-range dependencies, allows for parallization and guarantees stationarity" ([1907.06673] §1), and avoids "exponentially vanishing and exploding gradients through time... one of the main issues why RNNs are difficult to optimize" (§3.2). An unconstrained GRU with a tanh output head and no learned level metadata can carry an arbitrary drift.
- **Training instability as the proximate cause.** Improper learning-rate/batch-size choices are analytically linked to explosion in GAN training on financial data ([2112.00222]: explicit optimal adaptive LR and batch size, "revealing a relation between improper choices of learning rate and explosion in GANs training", and "analytical support for the popular practice of clipping"). Separately, WGAN-GP with a *finite* number of critic updates per generator update is shown **not** to converge in general ([1801.04406]).
- **Non-stationarity: averaging regimes.** Financial windows come from different regimes; an unconditional GAN trained on bull+bear windows must fit one distribution to several — the drift/mean is where the compromise lands. MarketGAN's motivation is exactly this: covariate-conditional, time-varying factor coefficients are needed, and their empirical gains are concentrated in dependence, not marginals ([2601.17773] §1, §4.4–4.5).
- **Memorization on tiny data.** With very few samples, generators memorize instead of generalize (DG cites Arora & Zhang 2017 in this context, [1909.13403] §4.1); discriminator overfitting with limited data "typically leads to discriminator overfitting, causing training to diverge" ([2006.06676]). With ~63–140 windows, this regime is the *default*, not the edge case.

### 1.2 (b) Under-produced autocorrelation (i.i.d.-looking outputs)

- **Sequence structure is not what adversarial loss enforces.** "Off-the-shelf techniques, such as GANs, struggle to capture the temporal dependence of joint probability distributions induced by time-series data" ([2111.01207] §1). In the same paper, a WGAN-GP with an LSTM generator "struggles to capture the temporal covariance of the volatility data" (§5.2.1), and correlation-metric error *grows* with the number of time steps for LSTM generators (§5.1.2, Table 2) — i.e., the longer the window, the more the sequence structure is averaged away.
- **The critic washes out per-step/per-variable structure when it judges jointly.** [2410.09850] documents the analogue in the cross-section: "the discriminator does not separately distinguish between the two variables but instead provides feedback on both variables together. Consequently, the information on x2 with larger volatility may have been offset" (§4.3.2). The same joint-feedback averaging applies across *time steps*: a critic scoring a whole window can be satisfied by a window with correct marginals but scrambled dynamics.
- **RNN pass count again.** With S=1 sequential generation over hundreds of bars the RNN forgets; batch generation (S records per pass) is the fix that "gives substantial improvements in signal quality" even for "a small (but larger than 1) S" ([1909.13403] §4.1).
- **Classical baselines capture it because it is their design.** DoppelGANger notes even standard ML/AR/Markov baselines "fail to capture weekly and/or annual patterns" in WWT (§2.2) — but for *returns*, GARCH-family models capture the ACF "quite well... the GARCH structure was designed to capture this dependence" ([1907.06673] §7.4.3). This is the honest framing of the user's 0.02-vs-0.69 gap: the parametric baseline wins on ACF by construction, not by luck.

### 1.3 (c) Collapsed cross-asset correlation

- **Documented in a controlled experiment.** On bivariate correlated GBM (true ρ≈0.9), a vanilla MLP GAN produced ρ=0.8249; more tellingly the *bivariate distribution turned circular* ("while the high correlation is well described in the original bivariate distribution that extends in one direction, the generated bivariate distribution forms a circular shape"), and the two generated marginals "look alike" — the GAN modeled only the lower-volatility variable ([2410.09850] §4.3.2). This is the same "corr dist 4.6" signature, with the mechanism named: a critic that judges the joint without per-coordinate structure.
- **Breaking it is easy, silently.** "Generating independent time series for each measurement dimension will break their correlations" ([1909.13403] §3.3.2) — the flat warning for per-symbol generators.
- **The fix that has empirical support.** Generate the full cross-section as a **single joint vector** under an explicit factor-model inductive bias: MarketGAN (98 S&P-100 assets, daily returns, WGAN-GP + TCN) reports that this "substantially improve[s] the fidelity of cross-sectional dependence, including dependence in extreme returns" versus factor-model bootstrap, with covariance estimates that win downstream mean–variance tests ([2601.17773] §1, §4.4–4.5, §5). CoMeTS-GAN similarly targets "explicitly modeling inter-asset correlation structures" for correlated stocks ([2605.27113]).
- **Temporal×spatial coupling.** Sig-WGAN's full correlation metric (Σ over all s,t,i,j of |ρ_real − ρ_gen|) is exactly the object that "corr dist" approximates, and its reported result is that a WGAN-GP cannot drive it down on rough-volatility data while a signature-based critic can ([2111.01207] §5).

### 1.4 (d) Under-sampled tails

- **Gaussian priors have finite moments.** For an SVNN/neural process driven by Gaussian noise, all moments exist; "since financial time series are understood to exhibit heavy-tails, we show that the Lambert W transformation plays an essential role for generating heavy-tailed stock price returns by using methods for normalized Gaussian data" ([1907.06673] §1, §5.3; "for δ>0 the Lambert W × F_X transformed random variable has heavier tails than X" §5.3). The paper also warns that moving to heavier-tailed *latent* inputs to fix this "may come at the cost of optimization instabilities" (§5.2) — so the transformation belongs in the data pipeline, not the latent.
- **Measured tail deficits in working systems.** MarketGAN reproduces heavy tails but "does not perfectly replicate the most extreme marginal tail events" (median kurtosis ≈10 not fully reproduced, [2601.17773] §4); Heston vol-of-vol generations go "more platykurtic than the original data" ([2410.09850] §4.3.1). Tail fidelity is the *last* thing to converge in every reported finance GAN.
- **Mode collapse = tail loss.** Tail events are rare modes; mode collapse (range-variability version, §1.1) deletes them before anything else. Minibatch discrimination was introduced precisely to fight mode collapse ([1606.03498]).
- **If tails matter, train for them.** Tail-GAN replaces the plain adversarial objective with one built on the joint elicitability of VaR and ES, and reports correctly captured tail risk for a broad class of trading strategies, with PCA scaling it to high-dim multi-asset inputs ([2203.01664]).

## 2. Known-good fixes, ranked by empirical support

Ranking = reported effect size × reproducibility (official code / published hyperparameters) × relevance to this setup.

| # | Fix | What it changes | Why it works for time series | Reported hyperparameters / results |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **DoppelGANger auto-normalization** | Per-series normalization; min/max generated as "fake" metadata; rescale after | Removes the range-variability mode collapse that produces wrong levels/means (§1.1) | WWT: generated samples regain diverse amplitudes/offsets/shapes (Fig. 5 of [1909.13403]); DG on ~500 samples still beats baselines trained on 50,000 on autocorrelation MSE (DG §5, as verified in this repo's chinese-lab-rl-foundations.md) |
| 2 | **TimeGAN supervised loss (embedder/supervisor/recovery + adversarial)** | Next-step embedding prediction loss alongside GAN loss; G and D in embedding space | The supervised term is the only mechanism that explicitly enforces one-step dynamics — i.e., short-lag autocorrelation | Official recipe: GRU, 3 layers, hidden 24, batch 128, 50k iterations, seq_len 24 (github.com/jsyoon0823/TimeGAN README); evaluated via discriminative + predictive scores |
| 3 | **Batch generation, S records per RNN step** (already on) | Cuts RNN passes by S | Fewer passes = less temporal forgetting; intra-batch rows share one hidden state, so short-lag structure is generated, not chained | "S=5 works well"; rule: T/S ≈ 50 steps ([1909.13403] §4.1, §4.4); S=1 is what RCGAN/TimeGAN-era generators did (§4.4) |
| 4 | **TTUR (two time-scale update rule)** | Separate learning rates for G and D | Proves convergence to local Nash equilibrium; D at higher rate keeps the critic ahead (the WGAN prescription was 5 critic steps per G step [1701.07875]) | [1706.08500]: TTUR improves DCGAN and WGAN-GP on CelebA/CIFAR-10/SVHN/LSUN; also introduced FID. (ganhacks #11: don't hand-tune G:D step schedules — use principled ratios) |
| 5 | **Spectral normalization (SN)** | Normalize each layer's weight matrix by its spectral norm (Lipschitz 1) | Stabilizes the critic; later analysis proves SN controls *both* exploding and vanishing gradients, with no hyperparameters to tune | [1802.05957] (ICLR 2018); mechanism proof in [2009.02773] ("BSSN" variant included) |
| 6 | **Gradient penalty placement / R1** | WGAN-GP penalizes ‖∇D‖ on interpolated points, λ=10 ([1704.00028]); R1 penalizes ‖∇D(x_real)‖ | WGAN-GP "performs better than standard WGAN and enables stable training... with almost no hyperparameter tuning"; but [1801.04406] proves WGAN-GP with finite critic updates need not converge, and that **zero-centered (R1) penalties do converge** | λ=10 is the published default; R1 γ≈10 typical; instance noise also proven convergent ([1801.04406]) |
| 6b | **GAN-QP** | Replace WGAN's 1-Lipschitz-constrained critic with a quadratic-potential loss | "No gradient vanishing and no 1-Lipschitz constraint"; simpler than WGAN, claims better practice | [1811.07296]. (Note: GAN-QP is a *loss* family, not a window-based critic — see §3) |
| 7 | **Hinge loss** | Non-saturating margin loss used with SN in SN-GAN lineage | Keeps gradient signal to G alive when D is strong | Geometric GAN [1705.02894]; standard choice in the SN-GAN line — reasonable swap if critic saturates |
| 8 | **Feature matching** | G trained against intermediate D features of real data, not just D's final score | When D overfits, the generator still gets a target that is not degenerate | [1606.03498] — introduced alongside label smoothing (real=0.9, fake=0.1) and minibatch discrimination |
| 9 | **Label smoothing / noisy labels** | Soft real label 0.7–1.2, soft fake 0.0–0.3 | Damped D confidence → prevents D from going to zero-loss (saturation) | [1606.03498]; ganhacks #6 ("soft and noisy labels") |
| 10 | **Batch norm vs no-norm** | Norm choice in G and D | Evidence: batch norm is *unstable* in GANs and can hurt quality; weight normalization gives 10% lower reconstruction error (DCGAN, CelebA/LSUN/CIFAR) | [1704.03971]; ganhacks #4: never mix real and fake in one BN batch — use separate minibatches (crucial for your tiny-window batches, where stats are noisy) |
| 11 | **Adaptive augmentation (limited data)** | Data-augment real+fake consistently; adapt strength | Directly targets the discriminator-overfitting divergence that dominates at 63–140 samples | [2006.06676]: "good results are now possible using only a few thousand training images"; non-saturating/augmentation > new losses at small n |
| 12 | **Weight averaging / checkpoints** | Average G weights over training (or keep best-checkpoint replay) | Standard RL-borrowed stability (experience replay of past G samples) | ganhacks #8 (replay buffers, checkpoint swapping); WALS-style averaging is the modern form |

**The ordering caveat.** "Are GANs Created Equal?" ([1711.10337], NIPS'18): across 6 losses × 7 datasets with equal tuning, most GAN objectives reach *similar* scores; "improvements can arise from a higher computational budget and tuning more than fundamental algorithmic changes", and none consistently beat plain non-saturating GAN. Translation for OpenTrader: loss choice (WGAN-GP vs hinge vs GAN-QP) is a **second-order** knob; architecture, conditioning, preprocessing, and data pipeline (fixes 1–3, 11) are first-order. Do not re-roll the objective as the first response to a failure.

## 3. Autocorrelation preservation: what actually works

Three families, with reported results:

1. **Batch generation (DoppelGANger)** — *best-documented for lag structure.* S records per RNN step; "even a small (but larger than 1) S gives substantial improvements in signal quality" (Fig. 4: autocorrelation MSE vs S, [1909.13403] §4.1). Reported outcome on the WWT dataset: DG reproduces weekly spikes + the ~1-year peak, with **91.2% lower mean-square error from the true autocorrelation than the closest baseline (RCGAN)** (§5.2). The user already has S=5 — the remaining gap in this dimension is the *critic* (next two bullets) and the metric itself (§6).
2. **Sequence-level discriminators with a supervised dynamics term (TimeGAN)** — the discriminator sees full sequences in embedding space, but the *supervisor* loss (one-step-ahead embedding prediction) is what demonstrably keeps dynamics: TimeGAN's whole evaluation protocol is discriminative + predictive scores on dynamics ([NeurIPS 2019], official repo). If only one change is made this run, it is this.
3. **Window/feature-based critics (signature, correlation, trend)** — SigWGAN replaces the learned critic with the **signature W₁ metric** (expected signature of the path; analytic, supervised, no min-max), and reports consistent wins over WGAN-GP on a full spatial×temporal correlation metric: e.g. 3-dim GBM with 100 timesteps, correlation metric ~2.7× better for the same LSTM generator; on rough-vol data the plain WGAN "struggles to capture the temporal covariance of the volatility data" ([2111.01207] §5). The conditional version (condition on past window) is [2006.05421]. The attention-based GAN of Fu–Hirsa–Osterrieder reports "the attention-based GANs not only reproduce the stylized facts, but also smooth the autocorrelation of returns" vs the QuantGAN convolutional baseline ([2207.00493]) — i.e., longer-receptive-field critics help ACF too.
4. **GAN-QP is not a window-based critic.** It is a loss construction (quadratic potential, no Lipschitz constraint, no gradient vanishing — [1811.07296]). If the user's "window-based critic" experiment is wanted, the literature-backed options are signature critics (#3), a critic over consecutive sub-windows with per-window scores averaged (an informal but common practice), or a critic that scores *pairs* of consecutive windows (enforces inter-window continuity). The last two are engineering, not literature — no published head-to-head exists; the published mechanism with numbers is #1–#3.

Bottom line on lag-1..5: batch generation (S=5) + a supervised one-step dynamics term is the combination with the strongest published autocorrelation results (DG 91.2% ACF-MSE reduction; TimeGAN's predictive score protocol). A pure adversarial loop — what the current run has — has no reported mechanism for short-lag fidelity.

## 4. Conditioning on a regime label (bull/bear/crisis)

- **The canonical cGAN rule: condition *both* networks.** "Simply feeding the data, y, we wish to condition on, to both the generator and discriminator" ([1411.1784]). Omitting the label from the critic is not the standard design.
- **Embedding vs one-hot.** At 3 classes it barely matters; the practitioner canon is embeddings for categorical conditioning (ganhacks #16: "Use an Embedding layer... keep embedding dimensionality low"), and SN-GAN-style conditional models use class embeddings in G and D ([1802.05957] line). One-hot is acceptable at K=3; an embedding of dim ≈ K gives the same gradient behavior with a learnable distance between regimes.
- **Where to inject.** DoppelGANger's design — the only one with strong reported metadata-conditioning results — feeds the metadata vector (its MLP output) into the generator at *every RNN step*, not just at the latent input ([1909.13403] §4.4, Fig. 7). Injecting only at the first step is the common mistake; every-step injection is what makes the condition actually shape dynamics. MarketGAN conditions its coefficient generators on covariates at every temporal position in the receptive window ([2601.17773] §3.1).
- **The label's marginal must be enforced too — that is what the auxiliary discriminator is for.** DoppelGANger's metadata-only Wasserstein critic (α-weighted) exists because jointly generating metadata+series "over-concentrates the discriminator's judgment" and metadata fidelity collapses ([1909.13403] §4.3). With a regime label, the auxiliary critic enforces the label's distribution, and the series critic judges within-label structure. Keep α as the balance knob. (The user already has the auxiliary critic — route the regime label through *it*, in addition to feeding G.)
- **Leakage warning.** If the regime label is computed from the *same* window that is being generated (e.g., "bull if SPY > 96d MA" evaluated inside the window), the condition is post-hoc; condition on the *lagged* state (regime at window start, or at t−1) so the label is genuinely prior information. This is the standard practice implied by conditional/window-conditioned generators ([2006.05421] conditions on the *past* window; MarketGAN on lagged covariates).

## 5. Scale and pragmatics for 17 dims × ~1250 bars

**The honest constraint: ~63–140 windows is very few samples for a GAN.** The limited-data literature is unambiguous that the binding failure at this size is discriminator overfitting → divergence ([2006.06676]), and that memorization replaces generalization (DG citing Arora & Zhang 2017, [1909.13403] §4.1). Consequences:

- **More overlapping windows (already done) is the right lever, and it is literature-backed.** DAT-CGAN "improve[s] sample efficiency through an overlapped block-sampling method" ([2009.12682]); QuantGAN's preprocessing pipeline literally ends in "Step 5: Rolling Window Cast" ([1907.06673] §5.3 pipeline figure). Overlap is the standard way to inflate a single long series into a dataset. Block bootstrap is a reasonable additional augmentation, but it does not add information; GAN training benefits come from *more distinct windows* (stride < window-length is already doing that) plus **augmentation of real and fake consistently** ([2006.06676]'s mechanism).
- **Train the 17 symbols jointly (they are the cross-section, not separate datasets).** MarketGAN's whole thesis is that the joint return vector is the object of interest and that per-asset generation is what loses correlations ([2601.17773]). Do not add samples by treating symbols as independent series.
- **Model budget: published sizes are tiny.** DoppelGANger: 1-layer LSTM, 100 units; both discriminators 4-hidden-layer MLPs with 200 units; Adam lr 1e-3, batch 100, GP λ=10 ([1909.13403] §5.3 — verified from the paper's setup text). TimeGAN: GRU 3 layers, hidden 24, batch 128 ([NeurIPS 2019] repo). Both are seconds-per-epoch on a consumer GPU. The current GRU design is in-budget; if the drift persists after fixes #1–#2, the architecture move with published support is **TCN generator (stationary by construction, no BPTT-through-generation)** ([1907.06673] §3.2, §4.2) — with the caveat that generator architecture choice empirically dominates GAN quality on financial data and there is no universally best one ([2410.09850] conclusion).
- **GAN vs parametric/copula — the evidence both ways:**
  - **For GANs:** QuantGAN's TCN/SVNN "outperforms a typical GARCH-model with respect to distributional and dependence properties" — GARCH matches the returns ACF "quite well" (it was designed for it) but fails tails (normal assumption) and "the leverage effect is not captured at all" ([1907.06673] §1, §7.4.3). Deep Hedging: "network-based generators outperform classical methods on a range of benchmark metrics, and adversarial training achieves the best performance" for *multivariate* financial series ([1911.01700]). MarketGAN beats factor-model bootstrap on cross-sectional dependence and downstream covariance estimation ([2601.17773]).
  - **Against GANs / for simpler models:** vanilla GANs are "very limited in capturing the dependencies between multivariate time series" ([2410.09850] §5); "no model yet satisfies all the stylized facts" (diffusion-time-series paper's own survey statement, [2410.18897] abstract); GANs are "challenging to train and sensitive to data limitations, particularly in high-dimensional and noisy environments such as financial markets" ([2601.17773] §2.1); and at 1250 bars, the existing regime-switching jump-diffusion sampler already wins the ACF metric — GARCH-family models capture serial dependence by construction ([1907.06673] §7.4.3). The Assefa et al. ICAIF 2020 "opportunities, challenges and pitfalls" piece (cited in [2410.09850]) is the standard caution.
  - **Synthesis:** keep the parametric sampler as the benchmark (it is the right one); the GAN earns its place only on the dimensions the parametric model cannot hit (tails via Lambert W, leverage, cross-asset correlation). The best-supported structure at this data size is the **factor-conditional GAN** (economic inductive bias + joint vector) rather than a bare generator ([2601.17773] is the existence proof on 98 assets with ~6.5k in-sample bars).

## 6. Evaluation metrics and pitfalls

**The standard battery (all published, all cheap at this scale):**
- **TimeGAN discriminative score** — post-hoc RNN classifying real vs synthetic; **predictive score** — train a one-step-ahead model on synthetic, evaluate on real (lower MAE = better dynamics). These are the de-facto standard pair ([NeurIPS 2019], official repo; "train on synthetic, test on real" is also DoppelGANger's downstream protocol [1909.13403] §5.2).
- **Autocorrelation MSE** — MSE between the ACF of real and generated series (DoppelGANger's headline metric; QuantGAN's version: ACF *score* over S=250 lags, M=500 paths, T̃=4000 bars, computed separately for **returns, squared returns, absolute returns**, plus a leverage-effect score [1907.06673] §7.4).
- **Distribution distances** — EMD/Wasserstein on return distributions (QuantGAN), JSD on log-return and final-value distributions ([2410.09850] §4.2), Sliced-Wasserstein + Mahalanobis + DTW (MarketGAN's quantitative battery, [2601.17773] §4.5).
- **Full correlation matrix distance** — Σ_{s,t,i,j} |ρ(X_s^i,X_t^j) − ρ(X̂_s^i,X̂_t^j)| (Sig-WGAN's correlation metric [2111.01207] §5); MarketGAN's Frobenius-norm correlation-matrix error, used as the *model-selection criterion* during training ([2601.17773] §4.2) — select checkpoints on this, not on the critic loss.
- **Tail metrics** — kurtosis/EVT-style comparisons (MarketGAN reports the kurtosis ≈10 gap as the residual weakness, [2601.17773] §4.4); elicitability-based VaR/ES scoring (Tail-GAN [2203.01664]).

**Pitfalls when interpreting:**
1. **Distance metrics are dominated by drift/level terms.** Final-value JSD blows up with length *even when return marginals match* (error accumulation, [2410.09850] §4.3.1); Sig-WGAN's toy example shows W₁ increasing with the drift gap ([2111.01207] §4.1). Consequence: a "distribution distance" computed on level paths or cumulative returns reports the 12× drift and hides everything else; compute distances on **standardized returns**, and report the mean separately. Also normalize before computing ACF-MSE, or the drift leaks into lag-0.
2. **Single scalar metrics are uninformative about mode collapse.** "Existing metrics yield uninformative or contradictory results"; the fix is separate **precision (quality) and recall (coverage)** estimates ([1904.06991], NeurIPS 2019). A 4.6 correlation distance and a 0.69 ACF error both pass a marginal-distance test — always report coverage (mode count, tail frequency) alongside.
3. **Conclusions are confounded by tuning budget.** With equal tuning, most GAN objectives perform alike ([1711.10337]) — any claim "fix X beat the baseline" is only meaningful with matched hyperparameter search on both sides. The parametric baseline at 0.02 ACF-error is your tuning-free reference point; treat it as the floor.
4. **Discriminative scores saturate/understate on tiny data.** A classifier can memorize 63–140 windows; a perfect discriminative score on training windows proves nothing. Use hold-out windows and report the split ([2006.06676]'s overfitting diagnosis applies to the *evaluator* too).
5. **Metric variance at n≈100 is huge.** Bootstrap confidence intervals on every reported distance; a 0.69-vs-0.02 ACF difference is far outside noise, but the differences *between* candidate fixes will not be — do not re-rank fixes on single runs.

- **Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data: Challenges, Initial Promise, and Open Questions** (DoppelGANger) — arXiv **1909.13403** (IMC 2020) — §3.3.2 failure modes + range-variability mode collapse, §4.1 batch generation (S=5, T/S≈50, "more passes → more forgetting"), §4.2 auto-normalization (min/max as fake metadata), §4.3 auxiliary metadata discriminator, §4.4 every-step metadata injection, §5.2 91.2% ACF-MSE reduction vs RCGAN + 43% downstream accuracy, §5.3 hyperparameters (LSTM 1×100, MLP D 4×200, λ=10, Adam 1e-3, batch 100).
- **Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks** (TimeGAN) — NeurIPS 2019 proceedings, papers.nips.cc/paper/8789 (Yoon, Jarrett, van der Schaar) — **no arXiv version; the task-brief ID 1909.11659 is a physics paper (verified on the arXiv abs page)** — embedder/supervisor/recovery/adversarial losses; discriminative + predictive scores; official repo github.com/jsyoon0823/TimeGAN (GRU, 3 layers, hidden 24, batch 128, 50k iterations, seq_len 24).
- **Quant GANs: Deep Generation of Financial Time Series** — arXiv **1907.06673** (Quantitative Finance 2020) — §3.2 TCNs (receptive-field formula; "guarantees stationarity"; no vanishing/exploding gradients through time), §5.3 Lambert W × F_X tail preprocessing (δ>0 heavier tails; heavy-tailed latent "may come at the cost of optimization instabilities"), §7.4 ACF/leverage/EMD protocol (S=250, M=500, T̃=4000) and GARCH(1,1) comparison (outperformed on distributional + dependence; GARCH good at ACF "by design", fails tails + leverage).
- **Improved Training of Wasserstein GANs** (WGAN-GP) — arXiv **1704.00028** — gradient penalty on interpolates, λ=10, "almost no hyperparameter tuning".
- **Wasserstein GAN** — arXiv **1701.07875** — 5 critic steps per generator step; clipping failure mode (cited via [1704.00028]).
- **Spectral Normalization for Generative Adversarial Networks** — arXiv **1802.05957** (ICLR 2018).
- **Why Spectral Normalization Stabilizes GANs: Analysis and Improvements** — arXiv **2009.02773** — SN controls exploding *and* vanishing gradients; explains why the standard SN needs no tuning.
- **GANs Trained by a Two Time-Scale Update Rule Converge to a Local Nash Equilibrium** (TTUR) — arXiv **1706.08500** (NIPS 2017) — separate LRs; introduced FID.
- **Which Training Methods for GANs do actually Converge?** — arXiv **1801.04406** (ICML 2018) — WGAN-GP with finite critic updates need not converge; zero-centered (R1) penalties and instance noise do.
- **Improved Techniques for Training GANs** — arXiv **1606.03498** (NIPS 2016) — feature matching, minibatch discrimination, label smoothing, historical averaging.
- **On the Effects of Batch and Weight Normalization in Generative Adversarial Networks** — arXiv **1704.03971** — BN unstable in GANs; WN 10% lower reconstruction error.
- **Training Generative Adversarial Networks with Limited Data** — arXiv **2006.06676** (NeurIPS 2020) — discriminator overfitting at low n; adaptive augmentation.
- **GAN-QP: A Novel GAN Framework without Gradient Vanishing and Lipschitz Constraint** — arXiv **1811.07296** — loss construction, not a window critic.
- **Conditional Generative Adversarial Nets** — arXiv **1411.1784** — condition fed to both G and D.
- **Can GANs Learn the Stylized Facts of Financial Time Series?** — arXiv **2410.09850** — bivariate GBM correlation collapse (0.899→0.825; "circular" bivariate distribution; discriminator joint-feedback mechanism), final-value JSD vs length (error accumulation), platykurtic Heston vol-of-vol, generator-architecture dependence (MLP/MLP-CNN/LSTM/GRU/TCN), hyperparameter sensitivity to G/D learning rates.
- **MarketGANs: Multivariate financial time-series data augmentation using generative adversarial networks** — arXiv **2601.17773** — factor-conditional GAN, single joint return vector, 98 S&P-100 assets, WGAN-GP+TCN; beats factor bootstrap on cross-sectional dependence + tail co-movement; kurtosis≈10 residual gap; FID/SWD/MD/DTW + correlation-Frobenius model selection; 7:1 split with fine-tuning; WGAN-GP motivation quote.
- **Sig-Wasserstein GANs for Time Series Generation** — arXiv **2111.01207** (ACM ICAIF 2021) — "GANs struggle to capture the temporal dependence..."; signature-W₁ critic; correlation metric definition; WGAN-GP struggles with temporal covariance of volatility; LSTM correlation error grows with timesteps; Logsig-RNN generator.
- **Conditional Sig-Wasserstein GANs for Time Series Generation** — arXiv **2006.05421** (Mathematical Finance special issue) — conditioning on the past window; conditional signature metric.
- **Tail-GAN: Learning to Simulate Tail Risk Scenarios** — arXiv **2203.01664** (Management Science) — VaR/ES elicitability objective; PCA for high-dim scalability.
- **Decision-Aware Conditional GANs for Time Series Data** (DAT-CGAN) — arXiv **2009.12682** — overlapped block-sampling for sample efficiency.
- **Simulating financial time series using attention** — arXiv **2207.00493** — attention GANs vs QuantGAN; autocorrelation smoothing.
- **Generative Adversarial Networks in finance: an overview** — arXiv **2106.06364** (Eckerli & Osterrieder) — survey; three architectures evaluated on financial series.
- **Convergence of GANs Training: A Game and Stochastic Control Methodology** — arXiv **2112.00222** (Mounjid & Guo) — LR/batch-size explosion relation for financial GANs; support for clipping.
- **Deep Hedging: Learning to Simulate Equity Option Markets** — arXiv **1911.01700** (NeurIPS 2019 RAIF workshop) — network generators > classical methods; adversarial best on multivariate series.
- **High-Quality Synthetic Financial Time-Series using a GAN-Diffusion Framework** (CoMeTS-GAN) — arXiv **2605.27113** — C-GAN for correlated stocks; explicit inter-asset correlation structures.
- **Generation of synthetic financial time series by diffusion models** — arXiv **2410.18897** — "no model yet satisfies all the stylized facts" (survey statement).
- **Are GANs Created Equal? A Large-Scale Study** — arXiv **1711.10337** (NIPS'18) — with enough tuning most losses tie; budget/tuning dominate.
- **Improved Precision and Recall Metric for Assessing Generative Models** — arXiv **1904.06991** (NeurIPS 2019) — separate quality/coverage; single metrics "uninformative or contradictory".
- **How to Train a GAN? Tips and tricks** (Soumith Chintala et al., practitioner canon, github.com/soumith/ganhacks) — input normalization + tanh, spherical Z, separate real/fake BN minibatches, leaky ReLU, soft/noisy labels (0.7–1.2 / 0.0–0.3), instance noise, embeddings for categorical conditioning, replay buffers, "don't balance loss via G:D schedule statistics". Author-flagged as not maintained since ~2020 — treat as experience-level corroboration, not evidence.
- **Real-valued (Medical) Time Series Generation with Recurrent Conditional GANs** (RCGAN) — arXiv **1706.02633** — DG's closest baseline (ID verified via the reference list of [2410.09850]).
- **Assefa et al., "Generating synthetic data in finance: opportunities, challenges and pitfalls"** — ACM ICAIF 2020 (DOI 10.1145/3419457.3430310), referenced from [2410.09850] — standard caution for financial synthetic data.

## Access notes (verify manually)

- **TimeGAN arXiv ID:** the brief's `1909.11659` resolves to "Duality family of scalar field" (physics.gen-ph) on arXiv — verified directly. TimeGAN is NeurIPS 2019 proceedings only; use papers.nips.cc/paper/8789 + the official repo.
- **GAN-QP:** is a loss family (1811.07296), not the "discriminator over consecutive sub-windows" mechanism the brief implies; no published head-to-head exists for sub-window/trend critics — that design space is engineering judgement, and §3 says so explicitly.
